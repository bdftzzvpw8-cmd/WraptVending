What to know before you call — read this first

How to describe us: "We're a vending machine operator. We own about ten refrigerated smart vending coolers that we place inside other businesses — gyms, hotels, offices, apartments — around Middle Tennessee. Owner-operated, no employees."

We're buying two things. First, general liability insurance — this protects the business if someone gets hurt or property gets damaged in connection with a machine or something it sold (a leak ruins a host's floor, someone trips on the machine, a customer claims a drink made them sick). Every hotel and property manager will require us to have this before they let a machine in the door.

The coverage amount to ask for: one million dollars per occurrence, two million dollars aggregate. In plain English: the insurer pays up to $1 million for any single incident, and up to $2 million total across all incidents in a year. This is the standard amount business landlords expect — don't accept less to save a few dollars.

Second, equipment coverage. The insurance industry calls this "inland marine" coverage — ignore the weird name, it simply means your business equipment is protected while it sits somewhere other than your own building. That's exactly our situation: our coolers live at host businesses. It covers theft, vandalism, fire, and water damage. Tell them: about $25,000–$30,000 worth of machines, spread across up to ten locations in Tennessee.

Two questions to ask every company: ① "Are certificates of insurance free and instant?" — a certificate of insurance is the one-page proof-of-coverage document hosts will ask us for; we'll need to produce one quickly and often. ② "Can I add a host business as an additional insured at no charge?" — "additional insured" means the host's name gets listed on our policy, which hotels and property-management companies frequently require before signing.

One thing to confirm: that the general liability policy includes products coverage — meaning it covers claims about the food and drinks we sell, not just accidents around the machine.

If a quote comes back far above $1,100/year, say you have lower quotes elsewhere — or just take the better one. Nothing here is negotiable-in-person magic; the online quotes are the real prices.
